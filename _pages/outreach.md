I strongly believe that mathematics is a deeply social endeavor. Collaborating with others is one of the most rewarding aspects of the discipline, and I believe that diverse communities lead to diverse ideas. As a first-generation, low-income (FGLI), Mexican, lesbian mathematician, I'm always happy to talk about my experiences if it helps someone feel that they belong in mathematics.

Throughout my undergraduate years at UC Berkeley, I sought opportunities to make mathematics more accessible and engaging for students. I volunteered with the [Berkeley Math Circle](https://mathcircle.berkeley.edu/) and in Oakland classrooms, where I worked with K–12 students to encourage curiosity and confidence in mathematics.

During my first year at the University of Minnesota, I served as a mentor for the [Mathematics Project at Minnesota (MPM)](https://sites.google.com/view/mpm-umn/home), a week-long program that helps undergraduate students build community, explore mathematical topics, and learn about research opportunities. In Spring 2026, I also mentored a student through the [Directed Reading Program (DRP)](https://sites.google.com/umn.edu/mathdrp/home), where we studied topics in topology and analysis.

My perspective on mentoring and community is influenced by [Federico Ardila's Axioms for Inclusive Mathematics](https://fardila.com/):

> **Axiom 1.** Mathematical potential is equally present in different groups, irrespective of geographic, demographic, and economic boundaries.

> **Axiom 2.** Everyone can have joyful, meaningful, and empowering mathematical experiences.

> **Axiom 3.** Mathematics is a powerful, malleable tool that can be shaped and used differently by various communities to serve their needs.

> **Axiom 4.** Every student deserves to be treated with dignity and respect.
